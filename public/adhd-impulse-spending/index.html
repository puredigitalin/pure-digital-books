ADHD & Impulse Spending: Practical Ways to Add Friction

Impulse spending can be connected to novelty, urgency, emotion and easy access. Instead of relying only on self-control, the approach in The ADHD Money Reset is to redesign the path to purchase.

In this guide
  • Why impulse spending can feel so compelling
  • The 24-hour cart rule
  • Friction tools that make a purchase less automatic
  • Reduce the triggers
  • What to do after a slip

Why impulse spending can feel so compelling

The book describes spending as something that can sometimes function as medicine, control or avoidance, followed by a money-shame spiral. The goal is not to add shame; it is to understand the pattern well enough to change the environment around it.

The 24-hour cart rule

When you want something that is not essential, place it in a cart or wishlist and wait 24 hours before buying. The pause is a simple form of friction: the decision does not disappear, but the immediate reward is separated from the purchase.

Try this: Create a wishlist specifically for non-essential purchases. Revisit it after the waiting period instead of buying immediately.

Friction tools

  • Remove saved payment information.
  • Unsubscribe from promotional marketing.
  • Turn off shopping notifications.
  • Use a wishlist instead of immediate checkout.
  • Make the purchase require one extra step.

These are intentionally small interventions. The point is not to make spending impossible; it is to make automatic spending less automatic.

Reduce the triggers

The book's approach to marketing is practical: identify the messages, notifications and environments that repeatedly trigger purchases, then reduce your exposure. An unsubscribe day and a subscription skeleton are examples of this approach.

What to do after a slip

The recovery approach is zero-judgment: name what happened, label the issue, decide whether it is a gap or a system break, fix the gap or ask why it happened, and restart.

If the same problem happens repeatedly, the book's Rule of Three suggests treating it as a possible system-design issue rather than repeatedly blaming yourself.
