ADHD Money Management: Build Systems That Work With Your Brain

ADHD money management is not simply about becoming more disciplined. A practical approach is to design money systems around the way attention, novelty, urgency and memory actually work.

In this guide
  • Why ordinary money advice can feel difficult
  • The four systems: friction, automation, visibility and interest
  • A simple account architecture
  • Make money visible
  • A simple place to start

Why ADHD money management can feel difficult

The book's approach starts with a simple shift: money problems are not treated as a character flaw. The system is designed around an interest-based nervous system, where novelty, urgency, challenge and interest can affect attention.

That is why a money plan that depends on remembering every bill, resisting every impulse and repeatedly doing boring administrative tasks can be difficult to maintain.

The core idea: Stop asking willpower to do the job of a system.

The four systems

1. Friction

Friction creates a pause between an impulse and an action. The book uses tools such as the 24-hour cart rule, wishlists, removing saved payment information and unsubscribing from marketing triggers.

2. Automation

Automation moves recurring tasks outside your memory. The book's architecture uses a Bills Account, a Safety Vault and an Everything Else Account, with standing transfers on payday.

3. Visibility

Out of sight can become out of mind. The book recommends a visible daily money number, pots or buckets for categories, a 15-minute weekly Money Date and a visible Safety Vault.

4. Interest

Make boring money tasks more engaging through challenges, streaks, body doubling, novelty and deadlines. The book suggests a monthly Money Sprint as one practical example.

A simple account architecture

The book describes three buckets:

  • Bills Account: fixed expenses and bills.
  • Safety Vault: emergency savings and safety money.
  • Everything Else Account: everyday spending and flexible expenses.

On payday, automate a transfer to Bills based on your fixed costs plus a buffer, and automate a transfer to the Safety Vault. The exact amounts should be adapted to your real numbers.

Make money visible

A weekly 15-minute Money Date is designed to keep the system visible without turning money management into an all-day project. The goal is a short recurring check-in rather than a perfect budget.

A simple place to start

  1. List your recurring bills and due dates.
  2. Identify the money tasks you repeatedly forget.
  3. Choose one task to automate.
  4. Add one friction step to a common impulse purchase.
  5. Schedule a short weekly Money Date.
